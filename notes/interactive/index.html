Lesson A1 · lifecycle stage 0, framing

Which slot should we book?

Consult times are right-skewed, and running late is worse than sitting idle. So the right slot is not the average visit; it's the quantile set by how much worse an overrun is than idle time.

Lab 1

The cost of every slot

Each minute a visit runs past its slot costs \(c_{over}\); each idle minute costs \(c_{idle}\). Edit the visits or the costs and watch the cheapest slot move. The flat bottom is always where the share of visits below it equals \(\frac{c_{over}}{c_{over}+c_{idle}}\).

Lab 2

The pinball loss

Divide the slot cost by \(c_{over}+c_{idle}\) and you get the loss a quantile model minimises:

\[ L_q(y,\hat y) = \begin{cases} q\,(y-\hat y) & y \ge \hat y \;\;\text{(ran over)}\\ (1-q)\,(\hat y-y) & y < \hat y \;\;\text{(idle)} \end{cases} \]

The loss decides what the model predicts
Train withThe model learns
\((y-\hat y)^2\)the mean
\(|y-\hat y|\) (pinball, q = 0.5)the median
\(L_q\)the q-th quantile

Lesson A2 · lifecycle stage 1, collect

The simulated hospital

actual minutes = hidden rule × noise. A model can learn the rule; nobody can predict the noise. Because we wrote both, we know the best score any model could reach.

Lab 5

Multiplicative log-normal noise

Draw \(Z\) from a symmetric bell, then multiply the visit by \(e^Z\). Percentiles carry straight through \(e^Z\); the mean does not, which is why the centre is shifted to \(\mu=-\sigma^2/2\).

Lab 6

z-scores

A z-score counts standard deviations from the mean: \(z = \frac{x-\text{mean}}{\text{SD}}\). Every normal curve has the same shape in these units, so each z marks a fixed share below it. 0.8416 is a z-score, not a standard deviation.

Lab 7

The oracle vs a fixed slot

The oracle knows every visit's expected minutes but not its noise. Its score is the floor for any model. Numbers are computed from the 11,144 July–August visits in the simulation. Try a noisier hospital with σ.

Who runs late? By department and visit type
