Where it struggles

Visits on time, by department and for the new doctor

    Before going live

    Limits and next steps

    • Simulated data. The method is real; the numbers are from a simulated hospital. A real deployment retrains on the hospital's EMR export.
    • Changes after training. Dermatology visits got longer from 1 July; the model, trained on January–June, books them too short. Monthly retraining and a drift monitor fix this.
    • New doctors. A doctor with no history gets the department's typical slot until a few weeks of their visits exist, then the model is retrained.
    • LightGBM or XGBoost. On June data (A7) LightGBM scored slightly better at every safety level and is faster, so it went forward; the two book the same slot for over 90% of June visits. XGBoost is here for comparison. Switching to it because of the July–August numbers would mean choosing on the test months, which the test is meant to rule out.
    • The safety level is a business decision. P67, P75 or P80 trades patient waiting against doctor idle time; the product owner picks from the table above.
    • Safe rollout. Run in shadow mode first (predict, compare, don't book), keep 15 minutes as the fallback if the model is unavailable.

    Inputs used, all known at booking time: doctor, department, visit type, clinic, weekday, hour, patient age (or unknown), prior reports, interpreter, video consult. Models: LightGBM and XGBoost with the quantile objective, one model per safety level, both trained the same way; LightGBM was chosen over XGBoost and simple lookup tables on June data. Visit types offered: new and follow-up (report-review visits in the history still count in the results).
